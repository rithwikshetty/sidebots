Privacy policy

23 November 2026

Sidebots has no accounts and no ads. Your agents and everything you send them live on your own computer. This page lists everything that leaves it.

On your computer

Sidebots keeps your agents, their chats, their memory and the files you send them in a folder on your computer. Nobody else has a copy, including us.

To answer you, an agent sends your messages and whatever it works on to the company behind the program it runs on, under your own login or API key. That program is Claude Code, which sends them to Anthropic, or Codex, which sends them to OpenAI. Anthropic's privacy policy and OpenAI's privacy policy cover that.

Apps you connect

You can connect Gmail, Google Calendar and Notion, so that your agents can use them as you. You sign in on the app's own page, and its maker gives your computer a token for what you allowed there: reading and sending your mail, reading and changing your calendars, or reading and changing your Notion. Your computer keeps the token in Sidebots' folder and asks Google or Notion itself. We never receive the token, your mail, your calendars or your Notion.

What an agent reads from an app you connected is used only for what you ask of your agents. It goes to Anthropic or OpenAI as part of the agent's work, as above, and nowhere else. When you remove a connection in the app, your computer forgets its token. You can also take one back in your Google Account, or in Notion's settings.

Sidebots' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Sidebots doesn't use your Google data to develop, improve or train AI models, doesn't use it for ads, and doesn't sell it. Nobody at Sidebots reads it.

On your phone

The app keeps a copy of your chats on the phone, so they open at once and can be read offline. It keeps the keys that pair it with your computer in the phone's keychain. It uses the camera to scan your computer's code and take photos you send, your photos and files only when you pick them, and the microphone for dictation, which your phone turns into text itself.

Between your phone and your computer

Your phone reaches your computer through Cloudflare's network, at an address made for your computer. The connection is encrypted on the way. Cloudflare handles the traffic as it does for any site it serves, and Sidebots runs no code that reads or keeps it.

Notifications

Only the developer of an app can send its notifications, so your computer sends each one through a small service we run on Cloudflare, called the relay, which passes it to Apple. Your computer encrypts every notification with a key only your phone holds. The relay and Apple pass it along without being able to read it. Each one comes with the address Apple gave your phone for notifications, which the relay hands to Apple and doesn't keep. When you read a chat on another device, your computer tells your phone the same way, so its notifications go. That says only that something was read, and nothing of which chat or what was in it.

While an agent works, its avatar can show on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. Your phone draws it from the agent's name and picture, which stay on the phone. When the work ends, your computer tells your phone through the relay and Apple, in one word that isn't encrypted: replied, failed or resting. It says nothing of which agent it is or what it said.

The relay keeps, for each computer: a random id, the public half of a key your computer made, its address, and how many notifications it sent today. It forgets a computer that has been quiet for 60 days.

How much Sidebots is used

When Sidebots starts on your computer, and once a day after that, it tells the relay how much Sidebots is used there, so we can see whether people use Sidebots and what to work on next. It sends the version of Sidebots, how many agents and paired phones you have, and how many times your agents ran on each of the last seven days, counted by Claude Code or Codex and by replies, scheduled jobs and fresh starts. That is all of it. Nothing in it says who you are, what your agents are called or what anyone said.

The relay keeps those numbers beside your computer's random id. When it forgets a computer, the daily counts stay, with nothing to say whose they were.

To stop sending them, turn off Share usage counts in the Sidebots menu on your Mac. On a server you started yourself, run pnpm counts off. If you turn them on again, the counts start from then.
